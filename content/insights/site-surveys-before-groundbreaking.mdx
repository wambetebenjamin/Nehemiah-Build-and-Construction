A site survey turns assumptions into information. It gives the design and construction team a stronger basis for discussing levels, boundaries, existing features, access, and coordination.

## Bring the right questions

The survey scope should be agreed with the project professionals. Depending on the site, they may recommend topographical, boundary, utility, or geotechnical investigations.

## Use findings early

Survey results can inform layout, drainage discussions, setting out, and construction access. If an unexpected condition appears later, pause and seek the appropriate professional review rather than relying on guesswork.

## Keep records together

Store the survey, current drawings, revisions, and approvals in one agreed project record. A clear document trail makes it easier for owners, consultants, and contractors to work from the same information.

Survey requirements vary by site. Confirm the scope with a qualified surveyor and the relevant authorities.
